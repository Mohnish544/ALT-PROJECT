1. OVERVIEW OF DIABETES MELLITUS
----------------------------------
Diabetes mellitus (DM) is a group of metabolic diseases characterized by
hyperglycemia resulting from defects in insulin secretion, insulin action, or both.

Main Types:
- Type 1 Diabetes (T1DM): Autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells.
  Absolute insulin deficiency. Typically onset in childhood/young adulthood.
  Accounts for ~5–10% of all diabetes cases.

- Type 2 Diabetes (T2DM): Progressive insulin resistance with relative insulin
  deficiency. Most common form (~90–95% of cases). Often associated with
  obesity, sedentary lifestyle, and genetic predisposition.

- Gestational Diabetes (GDM): Glucose intolerance first recognized during pregnancy.
  Increases risk of T2DM in both mother and child later in life.

- Other specific types: MODY (maturity-onset diabetes of the young), secondary
  diabetes (due to pancreatitis, Cushing syndrome, acromegaly, medication-induced).

Prediabetes:
  Fasting glucose: 100–125 mg/dL (impaired fasting glucose)
  2-hour OGTT:    140–199 mg/dL (impaired glucose tolerance)
  HbA1c:         5.7–6.4%

2. DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA
-----------------------
Diagnosis of diabetes requires one of the following:

  a) Fasting plasma glucose (FPG) ≥ 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L)
     (No caloric intake for ≥ 8 hours)

  b) 2-hour plasma glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) during
     75 g oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT)

  c) HbA1c ≥ 6.5% (48 mmol/mol)
     Performed in a certified laboratory

  d) Random plasma glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL with classic symptoms
     (polyuria, polydipsia, unexplained weight loss)

Note: In the absence of unequivocal hyperglycemia, criteria a–c should be
confirmed by repeat testing on a separate day.

3. GLYCEMIC MONITORING
-----------------------
HbA1c (Glycated Hemoglobin):
  - Reflects average plasma glucose over ~3 months.
  - Target: < 7.0% for most non-pregnant adults with T2DM.
  - More lenient target (< 8.0%): elderly, limited life expectancy,
    hypoglycemia unawareness, multiple comorbidities.
  - Tighter target (< 6.5%): short disease duration, long life expectancy,
    no significant hypoglycemia risk.
  - Test frequency: Every 3 months until stable; every 6 months when at goal.

Self-Monitoring of Blood Glucose (SMBG):
  - Fasting target: 80–130 mg/dL (pre-meal)
  - Postprandial target: < 180 mg/dL (2 hours post-meal)
  - Frequency: Individualized based on regimen and stability.
  - Essential for insulin-treated patients; helpful for lifestyle modification.

Continuous Glucose Monitoring (CGM):
  - Recommended for T1DM and intensive insulin-treated T2DM.
  - Provides time-in-range (TIR) data; target TIR ≥ 70% (70–180 mg/dL).
  - Reduces hypoglycemia and improves HbA1c when used consistently.

4. LIFESTYLE MANAGEMENT
------------------------
Medical Nutrition Therapy (MNT):
  - No single ideal eating pattern exists; individualize based on preferences.
  - General principles:
    * Reduce refined carbohydrate intake (white bread, sugary drinks, white rice)
    * Increase dietary fiber (vegetables, legumes, whole grains): ≥ 25 g/day
    * Limit added sugars (< 10% of total calories)
    * Choose healthy fats (mono- and polyunsaturated; limit saturated and trans)
    * Consistent carbohydrate distribution across meals for glucose stability
    * Caloric restriction for overweight/obese patients
  - Evidence-based dietary patterns:
    * Mediterranean diet — reduced cardiovascular risk
    * DASH diet — reduces blood pressure and improves glycemia
    * Low-carbohydrate diet — effective short-term for weight loss and HbA1c

Physical Activity:
  - Aerobic exercise: ≥ 150 min/week moderate intensity or ≥ 75 min/week vigorous
  - Resistance training: 2–3 times/week targeting all major muscle groups
  - Break prolonged sitting with light activity every 30 minutes
  - Benefits: Improves insulin sensitivity, reduces HbA1c by 0.5–1.0%,
    reduces cardiovascular risk, aids weight management

Weight Management:
  - 5–10% weight loss improves glycemic control significantly in T2DM
  - Intensive lifestyle intervention can achieve diabetes remission in some patients
  - Options: behavioral therapy, very low-calorie diet, bariatric surgery (BMI ≥ 40
    or BMI ≥ 35 with comorbidities)

Smoking Cessation:
  - Smoking increases risk of diabetes, comorbidities, and cardiovascular disease
  - All patients should be counseled and offered cessation support

5. PHARMACOLOGICAL TREATMENT FOR TYPE 2 DIABETES
--------------------------------------------------
Treatment Selection:
  - Consider efficacy, hypoglycemia risk, weight effects, cardiovascular outcomes,
    renal function, side effect profile, cost, and patient preference.

First-Line:
  a) Metformin (Biguanide)
     - Mechanism: Reduces hepatic glucose production; improves insulin sensitivity
     - Dose: Start 500 mg BID with meals; titrate to 2000 mg/day maximum
     - Benefits: Weight neutral, low cost, proven cardiovascular safety,
       possible mortality benefit (UKPDS)
     - Side effects: GI symptoms (nausea, diarrhea) in 20–30% — mitigated by
       slow titration and taking with food
     - Contraindications: eGFR < 30 mL/min (hold if < 45 for iodinated contrast),
       metabolic acidosis, severe hepatic impairment
     - Rare: Lactic acidosis (very rare with normal renal function)

  b) SGLT-2 Inhibitors (if CVD or CKD)
     Examples: Empagliflozin, Dapagliflozin, Canagliflozin
     - Mechanism: Block renal glucose reabsorption → glucosuria
     - Benefits: Weight loss (2–4 kg), BP reduction, HF hospitalization reduction,
       CKD progression slowing, CV mortality reduction (Empagliflozin — EMPA-REG)
     - Side effects: Urinary tract infections, genital mycotic infections,
       DKA (rare, more in T1DM), volume depletion
     - Contraindications: eGFR < 30 (< 45 for some agents)

  c) GLP-1 Receptor Agonists (if CVD or obesity)
     Examples: Semaglutide (Ozempic), Liraglutide (Victoza), Dulaglutide (Trulicity)
     - Mechanism: Mimic GLP-1 → increase insulin, suppress glucagon, slow gastric emptying
     - Benefits: Significant weight loss (2–5 kg), HbA1c reduction 1.0–2.0%,
       CV risk reduction (LEADER trial — Liraglutide)
     - Side effects: Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea (usually transient), medullary
       thyroid cancer risk (thyroid C-cell tumors in rodents; contraindicated
       with personal/family history of MEN2)
     - Injectable (weekly or daily) or oral (Semaglutide oral)

Other Drug Classes:
  d) DPP-4 Inhibitors (Sitagliptin, Saxagliptin, Alogliptin)
     - Weight neutral; low hypoglycemia risk; convenient once-daily dosing
     - Modest HbA1c reduction (~0.5–0.8%)

  e) Sulfonylureas (Glipizide, Glimepiride, Glyburide)
     - Inexpensive; stimulate insulin release
     - Significant hypoglycemia risk; weight gain; avoid in elderly/renal disease

  f) Thiazolidinediones (Pioglitazone)
     - Improve insulin sensitivity; NASH benefit
     - Weight gain, edema, bladder cancer risk (Pioglitazone), bone fracture risk

  g) Insulin Therapy
     - Required for T1DM; used when T2DM is uncontrolled on oral agents
     - Types:
       * Rapid-acting: Lispro, Aspart (onset 15 min; for mealtime use)
       * Short-acting: Regular insulin (onset 30–60 min)
       * Intermediate: NPH (onset 2–4 hrs)
       * Long-acting: Glargine, Detemir, Degludec (once daily basal)
     - Start with basal insulin: Glargine 10 units at bedtime, titrate by
       2 units every 3 days targeting FBG 80–130 mg/dL
     - Side effects: Hypoglycemia (most serious), weight gain, injection site reactions

Treatment Algorithm:
  Step 1: Metformin + lifestyle modification
  Step 2: Add SGLT-2i (if CVD/HF/CKD) or GLP-1RA (if CVD/obesity)
          or DPP-4i (if weight neutral preferred) or SU (cost a priority)
  Step 3: Triple therapy or add basal insulin
  Step 4: Intensive insulin regimen (basal-bolus)

6. COMPLICATIONS
-----------------
Microvascular:
  a) Diabetic Nephropathy:
     - Leading cause of end-stage renal disease (ESRD) in developed countries
     - Screening: Annual UACR and eGFR from diagnosis (T2DM) or 5 years (T1DM)
     - Microalbuminuria (UACR 30–300 mg/g): Early stage; ACEi/ARB protective
     - Macroalbuminuria (UACR > 300 mg/g): Progressive CKD

  b) Diabetic Retinopathy:
     - Most common cause of new blindness in working-age adults
     - Stages: Non-proliferative (NPDR) → Proliferative (PDR) → Diabetic macular edema
     - Screening: Annual dilated fundoscopy (T1DM: 5 years after diagnosis; T2DM: at diagnosis)
     - Treatment: Tight glycemic and BP control; anti-VEGF injections; laser photocoagulation

  c) Diabetic Neuropathy:
     - Distal symmetric polyneuropathy (DPN): Numbness, tingling, burning in feet
       ("glove and stocking" distribution)
     - Autonomic neuropathy: Orthostatic hypotension, gastroparesis, bladder dysfunction,
       erectile dysfunction
     - Screening: Annual monofilament testing + vibration assessment
     - Treatment: Glycemic control; Pregabalin, Duloxetine, or Gabapentin for pain

Macrovascular:
  - Coronary artery disease (2–4× increased risk vs. general population)
  - Peripheral arterial disease (PAD): Ankle-brachial index (ABI) screening
  - Stroke: 2× increased risk
  - Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death in T2DM patients

Diabetic Foot:
  - 15–25% of diabetic patients develop foot ulcers
  - Major cause of non-traumatic lower limb amputation
  - Prevention: Annual foot exam; proper footwear; patient education
  - Infection: Polymicrobial; prompt wound care and antibiotics

7. CARDIOVASCULAR RISK MANAGEMENT IN DIABETES
----------------------------------------------
- Lipid management: LDL target < 100 mg/dL (< 70 mg/dL if CVD present)
  Use statins for all patients with diabetes aged > 40 years.
- Blood pressure target: < 130/80 mmHg
- Antiplatelet therapy: Aspirin 75–162 mg daily if established CVD;
  consider for primary prevention only in high-risk patients
- SGLT-2 inhibitors and GLP-1 agonists with proven CV benefit preferred
  in patients with established CVD or high CVD risk

8. HYPOGLYCEMIA
---------------
Definition: Blood glucose < 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L)
Severe hypoglycemia: Requires assistance from another person for treatment

Symptoms:
  Adrenergic (early): Sweating, palpitations, tremor, anxiety, hunger
  Neuroglycopenic (late): Confusion, difficulty speaking, seizures, coma

Treatment (Rule of 15):
  - 15 g of fast-acting carbohydrate (4 glucose tablets, 120 mL orange juice, 1 tablespoon honey)
  - Wait 15 minutes; recheck glucose
  - Repeat if still < 70 mg/dL
  - Follow with a snack if next meal > 1 hour away
  Severe: Glucagon injection (IM/SC/IN) or IV dextrose (D50W) in hospital

Prevention:
  - Avoid skipping meals
  - Dose adjustments for exercise
  - Carry rapid-acting glucose at all times

9. SICK-DAY RULES
------------------
- Continue insulin and oral medications unless advised otherwise
- Monitor blood glucose every 2–4 hours
- Increase fluid intake to prevent dehydration and DKA
- Check urine ketones if glucose > 240 mg/dL
- Seek emergency care if: unable to keep fluids down, glucose > 300 mg/dL
  despite correction, ketones positive, altered mental status

10. DIABETES EDUCATION AND SELF-MANAGEMENT
-------------------------------------------
Diabetes Self-Management Education and Support (DSMES):
  - Recommended at diagnosis and annually thereafter
  - Core topics: nutrition, physical activity, blood glucose monitoring,
    medication management, problem-solving, coping, hypoglycemia management,
    sick-day rules, foot care, complication prevention

Healthcare Team:
  - Primary care physician or endocrinologist
  - Diabetes educator (CDE/CDCES)
  - Registered dietitian
  - Ophthalmologist
  - Podiatrist
  - Pharmacist
  - Mental health professional (depression is 2× more common in T2DM)
